## 1. Governing principle

JiTpro is a hands-on construction procurement and project-readiness service. It establishes early control over the information, decisions, approvals, products, materials, services and commitments that field execution depends on.

> **Identify what the field needs. Establish what must happen to make it ready. Assign responsibility. Control the sequence. Protect execution.**

Finding problems early is one benefit of this work, not the service being sold. The word *problem* stays wherever it is accurate. Real consequences such as delays, lost profit, rework, exhausted teams and preventable chaos are not softened. The goal is precision, not a gentler website.

## 3. Assessment of current positioning

**The body copy describes a control service; the headlines describe a problem-detection service.**

The detailed sections explain JiTpro accurately: requirements are normal, and JiTpro identifies them, assigns them, times them and keeps them moving. Those sections are:
- the homepage's *You may not call it procurement* section;
- the homepage audience tabs;
- Learn More and the methodology stages;
- the Owners page;
- the FAQ.

The lines a visitor reads first define JiTpro by finding problems:
- the About hero;
- the Founder Story's statement of purpose;
- four General Contractors headlines;
- the Owners *better way* heading;
- the first homepage benefit.

The nine high-priority changes fix those lines. Four further points from the original audit still stand:
- **Pain is used well.** It is specific and qualified with *preventable* or *avoidable*, and it stays.
- **Positive outcomes are often stated as the absence of problems.** Where a sentence says what JiTpro delivers, it should describe a state of readiness instead.
- **Involvement through construction is under-stated.** JiTpro stays with the project and gives weekly guidance, but the GC and Owners pages describe only up-front planning. M2, M3 and H7 address this.
- **The copy does not distinguish conditions.** *Problem* and *risk* cover everything from an open owner selection to a stopped crew. The approved vocabulary (Section 4) addresses this.

## 4. Messaging principles

1. **JiTpro's verbs are control verbs.**
   - Use: *identify, define, establish, assign, plan, sequence, track, guide, coordinate, protect*.
   - Do not make these JiTpro's main action: *uncover, discover, expose, detect, predict, see problems coming*.

   The field discovers problems. JiTpro makes sure there is less to discover.

2. **Call each condition by its name.** Approved by the founder (Q1):

   | Term | Meaning | Plain example |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | **Requirement** | Something the project needs to accomplish or provide | *The owner must choose the tile.* |
   | **Open item** | A requirement, decision or activity not yet completed | *The tile choice is still open.* |
   | **Constraint** | A condition that stops an activity from going ahead as needed | *The tile can't be ordered until it's chosen.* |
   | **Risk** | A possible future consequence if a condition is not dealt with | *If it isn't chosen by March, the floors may start late.* |
   | **Impact** | An actual consequence: interrupted work, rework or delay | *The tile crew lost two days.* |
   | **Commitment** | What must be done, who is responsible and when it must be complete | *The owner will choose the tile by 14 March.* |

   - **No forced progression.** These terms describe real conditions, and an open item does not have to become a constraint before it carries risk.
   - **An open item is not a problem.** An ordinary unresolved requirement is not automatically a problem. *Problem* is right for impacts and for industry pain.

3. **Pain first, then the plan.** Keep the hard truths. After each one, say what JiTpro establishes, not *we find problems*.

4. **Describe the result as a state, not an absence.** Prefer:
   - *the field gets what it needs when it needs it*;
   - *every requirement has an owner and a date*;
   - *ready for construction*.

   *Fewer preventable disruptions* is still right as a consequence.

5. **Say that the work continues.** JiTpro plans up front, then stays with the project, setting weekly priorities and looking ahead. Avoid wording that suggests an inspection, a report, and goodbye.

6. **Prevention is qualified, never promised.** Use *preventable* and *avoidable*. Never use *eliminate*, *guarantee* or an unqualified *prevents*.

7. **A service, supported by tools.** People do the planning and guidance; the contractor's team runs the job. Software-platform wording is left for its own future project (Section 9) and is not rewritten here.

8. **Plain words.** Short sentences in the contractor's own language, as the founder speaks. On the homepage, *procurement* stays out of new copy (Design System §20.1).
